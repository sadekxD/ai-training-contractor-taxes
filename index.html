Taxes for AI Training Contractors: US 1099 Basics, Plus UK and India Notes

Almost every AI-training job we track is contractor work. With a median advertised rate of $65/hr, 10 hours a week adds up to about $34,000 a year before tax, and nobody is withholding tax from it. In the US, that alone can mean a 15.3% self-employment tax on top of income tax. This is where a lot of first-year AI trainers get caught out.

This guide covers the basics for the US, UK and India. It's practical orientation, not tax advice. Rules change, and your situation may differ, so check with a local accountant or the official tax authority before you file.

First: you're almost certainly a contractor

Platforms like Mercor, micro1, Mindrift, Meridial and others engage you as an independent contractor (or through an employer-of-record such as Deel). That means:

  • No tax withheld from your payouts (in most cases)
  • No employer pension or social security contributions
  • You report the income yourself, and you can usually deduct legitimate business expenses

Check the platform review (for example Mercor or Mindrift) for how they pay: Payoneer, Wise, PayPal, Stripe or Deel. Your tax records start with those payout statements.

United States

The forms

  • W-9: you give this to the platform when you onboard.
  • 1099-NEC: platforms send this if they paid you over the reporting threshold. That's $600 for payments through 2025, rising to $2,000 for payments made from 2026, under recent legislation. Check the current IRS guidance.
  • 1099-K: may come from PayPal or other payment processors instead.
  • You owe tax on all income, whether or not you receive a form.

Self-employment tax

  • 15.3% (Social Security 12.4% plus Medicare 2.9%) on about 92.35% of your net self-employment earnings, once net earnings reach $400 or more.
  • Social Security applies only up to the annual wage base. Medicare has no cap.
  • You can deduct half of SE tax when calculating income tax.

Quarterly estimated payments

If you expect to owe $1,000 or more, the IRS expects quarterly estimated tax payments (Form 1040-ES). The usual due dates are 15 April, 15 June, 15 September and 15 January. Missing them can mean underpayment penalties.

Rough worked example (simplified)

Amount
Gross AI-training income $30,000
Business expenses (software, part of internet, equipment) –$1,500
Net profit $28,500

Plus federal and state income tax depends on your bracket

A common rule of thumb is to set aside 25–30% of every payout. Adjust it once you've done one year's return.

Deductible expenses (if used for the work)

  • A share of your internet and phone
  • A computer, headset or mic, used proportionally
  • Software subscriptions required by projects
  • A home office, if it's a regular and exclusive workspace (has strict rules)

United Kingdom

  • AI-training income is usually self-employed trading income.
  • The £1,000 trading allowance means gross trading income up to £1,000 a year doesn't need reporting. Above that, you'll generally need Self Assessment.
  • Register with HMRC by 5 October after the end of the tax year (6 April to 5 April) in which you started.
  • The online return and payment deadline is 31 January. Payments on account may apply after your first year.
  • You'll pay Income Tax plus Class 4 National Insurance on profits above the thresholds. Check current rates on gov.uk.
  • Making Tax Digital for Income Tax started in April 2026 for self-employed people with qualifying income over £50,000, and lower thresholds are planned for later years. If you're over the threshold, you'll need compatible software and quarterly updates.
  • Payments in USD should be converted to GBP using a consistent method, such as HMRC exchange rates or the actual rate received.

India

  • Freelance AI-training income is usually taxed as "profits and gains of business or profession."
  • Presumptive taxation: professionals may be able to use Section 44ADA, which treats 50% of gross receipts as taxable profit, within turnover limits (higher limits apply where cash receipts are minimal). Whether AI-training work counts as a "specified profession" is a judgment call, so ask a CA. Other freelancers may look at 44AD or regular books.
  • ITR form: typically ITR-3 or ITR-4, depending on the scheme you use.
  • Advance tax is due in instalments (June, September, December, March) if your liability is ₹10,000 or more.
  • GST: registration is generally required above ₹20 lakh of aggregate turnover. Services exported to foreign clients can be zero-rated under a LUT, but registration rules still apply above the threshold.
  • Keep your FIRA/FIRC (foreign inward remittance certificates) from your bank or Payoneer or Wise. They prove the export receipt.
  • Many US platforms will ask you for a W-8BEN. That form claims treaty status so US tax isn't withheld. It doesn't replace Indian tax.

Other countries, quickly

If you're in Kenya, Nigeria, the Philippines or elsewhere, foreign freelance income is generally taxable locally. In the Philippines, look at the 8% optional income-tax rate for eligible self-employed people. Kenya and Nigeria have their own registration (KRA PIN, TIN). Record-keeping that saves you at filing time

  • Monthly: export payout statements from every platform and payout app into one folder.
  • Track per platform: date, gross amount, fees and currency conversion.
  • Save receipts for anything you deduct.
  • Use one bank account for AI-training income if you can.
  • Keep records for at least 6 years. That covers the US, UK and India retention periods comfortably.

Honest caveats

  • Thresholds and rates change, sometimes mid-year. Everything above is current to the best of our knowledge as of October 2026, but verify it with the IRS, HMRC or the Income Tax Department, or a professional.
  • Some platforms pay through an employer-of-record, which can change how withholding works.
  • Platform fees, FX spreads and withdrawal fees are usually deductible business costs, so track them.
